Can a Credit Dispute Lower Your Credit Score?

A score change that happens during a dispute is not proof that filing the dispute caused it. Start with the underlying report data and make sure you are comparing the same scoring model.

Transaction dispute and account dispute are different

A transaction dispute challenges a particular charge with a card issuer. A credit-report dispute challenges information reported about an account. Confusing the two can make it harder to determine what actually changed.

Inspect the report, not only the number

Credit scores are calculated from report data using a particular scoring model. A displayed score can move when a balance, credit limit, account status, payment history, inquiry, collection, or other input changes. Consumer services may also show scores produced by different models.

Five questions to ask

  1. Did the account's reported status change?
  2. Was the whole account marked as disputed rather than one transaction?
  3. Did a balance or credit limit update?
  4. Did another account report new information at the same time?
  5. Are you comparing the same bureau data and scoring model?

Do not abandon a legitimate factual dispute merely because a consumer-app score moved temporarily. First determine whether the underlying information is accurate.

Sources: CFPB guide to reports and scores and CFPB dispute guidance.
