Debt Collector Calling? Here's What to Say

An unexpected caller asks for your name, address, or other personal information. You don't know them, and they called you. You are allowed to slow the conversation down and move it onto paper.

Pressure is not proof. Don't acknowledge an alleged debt, promise payment, or give an unsolicited caller personal information. Ask who they are, get the business information, and require written communication.

1. Start here

Caller: “Can I speak with [name]?”

You: “Who are you, what company do you represent, and what is the purpose of this call?”

3. Say this

You: “I do not provide information to unsolicited callers. Give me your name, your company's full legal name, mailing address, telephone number, and license number. I am not verifying anything for you.”

If they refuse to identify the business, end the call and document the refusal.

After the call

  • Write down the date, time, telephone number, caller's name, company, address, and exact statements.
  • Save any voicemail, text message, email, or letter.
  • When written information arrives, review it before doing anything else.
  • If you dispute the claim, respond in writing and use trackable mail.
  • Never ignore genuine court papers. A lawsuit has deadlines that a telephone call does not.
