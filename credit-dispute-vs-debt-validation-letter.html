Credit Dispute Letter vs. Debt Validation Letter

The names sound similar, but these letters go to different recipients and invoke different processes. Use the letter that matches the actual problem.

Credit-report dispute

A credit-report dispute challenges information you believe is inaccurate or incomplete in a consumer report. It may be sent to the credit-reporting company and to the business that furnished the disputed information.

Debt-validation dispute

A debt-validation dispute concerns a debt collector's collection claim. If you send a written dispute generally within 30 days after receiving the collector's validation notice, the collector generally must pause collection of the disputed debt until it sends verification responding to your dispute.

Which one fits?

  • Wrong balance or status on a credit report: credit-report dispute.
  • Account belonging to someone else: credit-report dispute and possibly identity-theft procedures.
  • Collector demanding payment for an unfamiliar or incorrect debt: debt-validation dispute.
  • The problem appears in both collection communications and a credit report: separate, properly addressed communications may be appropriate.

Documentation is the common denominator

Do not send a generic letter without explaining what you believe is wrong. Preserve the report, collection notice, envelopes, attachments, delivery records, and every response. A documented sequence makes it possible to show what happened later.

Sources: CFPB credit-report dispute guidance and CFPB debt-collection dispute guidance.
