What Is a 609 Dispute Letter?

A 609 dispute letter is a written request you send to a credit bureau — Equifax, Experian, or TransUnion — asking them to provide the documentation that supports a negative item on your credit report. The name comes from Section 609 of the Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA), 15 U.S.C. § 1681g, which gives you the legal right to request your credit file information and the sources behind it.

The idea is simple: if the bureau cannot produce documentation verifying an account, the item must be removed from your report under Section 611 of the same law.

What Section 609 Actually Says

Section 609 requires a credit reporting agency to disclose, upon request:

  • All information in your file at the time of the request
  • The sources of that information
  • A list of everyone who has received your credit report in the past two years (one year for employment inquiries)

Important distinction: Section 609 gives you the right to request information. The right to dispute and require removal of unverified items comes from Sections 611 and 623 of the FCRA. A strong 609 letter invokes both — requesting the documentation AND disputing the accuracy of items the bureau cannot verify.

When a 609 Letter Works (and When It Doesn't)

It works best when:

  • An account on your report belongs to someone else (mixed files, identity theft)
  • A debt collector reported an account they cannot fully document
  • Account details are wrong — wrong balance, wrong dates, wrong account number
  • The original creditor has gone out of business and records no longer exist
  • A debt has been sold multiple times and the paper trail is broken

It will not remove:

  • Accurate late payments that are fully documented by the original creditor
  • Legitimate debts that the creditor can verify
  • Public records (judgments, bankruptcies) that are correctly reported

The FTC found that 1 in 5 consumers had an error on at least one credit report. If you have errors, a 609 letter is one of the tools to get them corrected.

Free 609 Dispute Letter Template

Copy and customize this template. Replace the bracketed sections with your information. Send one letter per bureau, per disputed item.

[Your Full Name] [Your Address] [City, State ZIP] [Date] [Credit Bureau Name] [Bureau Address] Re: Request for Verification Under Section 609 of the FCRA (15 U.S.C. § 1681g) To Whom It May Concern: I am writing to exercise my rights under Section 609 of the Fair Credit Reporting Act. I am requesting that you provide documentation verifying the following account(s) currently reported on my credit file: Account Name: [Creditor Name] Account Number: [Full or Partial Account Number] Reason for Dispute: [e.g., "This account does not belong to me" / "The balance is incorrect" / "This account was paid in full"] Under Section 609(a)(1), I have the right to request all information in my consumer file and the sources of that information. Under Section 611, if you are unable to verify this information with original documentation from the data furnisher, this item must be removed from my credit report within 30 days. Please provide the original signed contract, payment history, or other documentation verifying this account. A generic response stating the information has been "verified" without providing the requested documentation does not satisfy the requirements of the FCRA. I have enclosed copies of my government-issued ID and a utility bill for identity verification. Sincerely, [Your Signature] [Your Printed Name] [SSN Last 4: XXX-XX-XXXX] Enclosures: - Copy of government-issued photo ID - Copy of utility bill or bank statement showing current address

How to Send It

Always send dispute letters by certified mail with return receipt requested. This gives you proof that the bureau received your letter and starts the 30-day clock for their response. Here are the mailing addresses:

  • Equifax: P.O. Box 740256, Atlanta, GA 30374-0256
  • Experian: P.O. Box 4500, Allen, TX 75013
  • TransUnion: P.O. Box 2000, Chester, PA 19016

Keep copies of everything you send. When the bureau responds, you will need to decide your next move — accept the result, send a follow-up letter, or escalate.

Beyond the 609: Other Letters You May Need

A 609 letter is just one tool. Depending on your situation, you may also need:

  • Debt Validation Letters — sent to collectors (not bureaus) under the FDCPA, demanding they prove you owe the debt
  • Method of Verification Letters — follow-up letters when a bureau claims they "verified" an item without showing you how
  • Escalation Letters — sent when bureaus fail to respond within 30 days or give generic responses
  • Sworn Denial / Legal Follow-Up Letters — for persistent items that should have been removed

Most credit repair situations require multiple letters in a specific sequence. The first letter rarely resolves everything — it is the follow-up process that gets results.
